This assignment helped me better understand how a pandas pipeline works and why data lineage matters. Before this assignment, I mainly thought about whether the final answer was correct, but now I understand why keeping the original data is also important. The part that challenged me most was putting the pipeline into the Streamlit app. I had to use a boolean index to filter rows for things like unmatched employees and overtime, and then use len() for counts and .sum() for totals. I also learned the difference between Series.apply and a row apply with axis=1. Series.apply works when transforming one value at a time, while axis=1 is useful when a calculation, such as gross pay, needs multiple values from the same row. Debugging the app also taught me to pay closer attention to the exact requirements of the assignment. I originally used a datetime conversion even though dates were supposed to remain as text, and I had parts of the app using payroll before a file had been uploaded. The automated tests helped me identify both problems. Finally, the export step showed me why the payroll table should remain unchanged while a separate DataFrame is created in the format required by the payroll provider. In future assignments, I will check the required grain, allowed methods, expected labels, and output format before writing each step so I can make the pipeline simpler and avoid unnecessary debugging.
